## Objective

Build a defensible valuation range, challenge the expectations gap, and identify downside paths, risk signals, and evidence that would invalidate the thesis.

## Workflow

1. Select peers by revenue exposure, unit economics, growth, margins, capital intensity, geography, and lifecycle; exclude convenient but economically weak matches.
2. Reconcile enterprise value, net debt or cash, obligations, diluted share count, and likely dilution before applying valuation methods.
3. Value base, bull, and bear cases with explicit forecast horizon, growth, margins, cash conversion, discount rate or multiple, and terminal assumptions.
4. Show sensitivities for the assumptions that drive the range. Use reverse valuation only when market price and comparable inputs support a meaningful implied-expectations test.
5. Build downside paths from filing risks, concentration, competition, regulation, execution, controls, litigation, liquidity, refinancing, and off-balance-sheet commitments.
6. Define catalysts, monitoring thresholds, and concrete thesis-invalidation evidence.

## Evidence

Use filings for obligations and risks, market data for capital structure and valuation date, and primary legal or regulatory records where applicable. Compare peer metrics on consistent periods and definitions. Seek evidence that contradicts both management's case and the analyst's preferred case.

## Return

Return atomic findings, peer inclusion logic, formulas, scenario assumptions, valuation ranges, sensitivities, risk owners or indicators, caveats, and unresolved inputs. Never present a single target as observed fact.
